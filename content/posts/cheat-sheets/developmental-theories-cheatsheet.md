# Developmental Theories — Cheat-Sheet

Kohlberg (moral) · Erikson (psychosocial) · Levinson (adult life structure)

---

## 1. Kohlberg's Stages of Moral Development

Three **levels**, each split into two **stages** (six total). Built on Piaget; assessed via moral dilemmas (classically the *Heinz dilemma*).

**Key principle:** stage is determined by the *reasoning* behind a judgement, not the judgement itself. Two people can reach opposite conclusions at the same stage, or the same conclusion at different stages.

| Level | Stage | Orientation | Basis of "right" |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I. Pre-conventional**<br>(morality is external; consequences to self) | 1 | Obedience & punishment | Avoid punishment; defer to power |
| | 2 | Instrumental / self-interest | "What's in it for me?" — reciprocal exchange ("you scratch my back…") |
| **II. Conventional**<br>(morality = social norms & approval) | 3 | Interpersonal accord ("good boy / good girl") | Being liked, meeting the expectations of those close to you |
| | 4 | Authority & social order ("law and order") | Upholding law, duty, and social order for their own sake |
| **III. Post-conventional**<br>(morality = self-chosen principles) | 5 | Social contract | Laws are mutable agreements serving the greater good; rights and relative values |
| | 6 | Universal ethical principles | Abstract, self-chosen principles (justice, dignity, equality); conscience overrides law |

**Caveats worth knowing (Kohlberg himself conceded several):**
- **Stage 6 is largely theoretical** — Kohlberg struggled to find empirical exemplars and at times floated a hypothetical *Stage 7* (a "cosmic"/transcendental orientation). Treat 6 as an ideal type.
- **Gilligan's critique:** the model privileges an *ethic of justice* (coded masculine in his sample) over an *ethic of care*, allegedly scoring women lower on a biased instrument.
- **Cultural bias:** the post-conventional stages reflect Western liberal individualism and travel poorly to collectivist contexts — directly relevant to any South African application.
- **Reasoning ≠ behaviour:** sophisticated moral reasoning predicts moral *action* only weakly.

## 2. Erikson's Stages of Psychosocial Development

Eight stages across the whole lifespan. Each is a **psychosocial crisis** — a tension between a *syntonic* (favourable) and *dystonic* (unfavourable) pole. Resolving it well yields a **basic strength (virtue)**; the poles are not "win/lose" but a workable *balance* tilted toward the favourable.

| # | Crisis | Approx. age | Virtue | Core question / task |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trust vs Mistrust | 0–18 mo (infancy) | **Hope** | "Is my world safe and dependable?" — caregiver reliability |
| 2 | Autonomy vs Shame & Doubt | ~1–3 yr | **Will** | "Can I do things myself?" — self-control, independence |
| 3 | Initiative vs Guilt | ~3–5 yr (play age) | **Purpose** | "Is it okay for me to act and plan?" — asserting agency |
| 4 | Industry vs Inferiority | ~5–12 yr (school age) | **Competence** | "Can I make it in the world of skills and tasks?" |
| 5 | Identity vs Role Confusion | ~12–18 yr (adolescence) | **Fidelity** | "Who am I, and where am I going?" |
| 6 | Intimacy vs Isolation | ~18–40 yr (young adult) | **Love** | "Can I form close, committed bonds?" |
| 7 | Generativity vs Stagnation | ~40–65 yr (middle adult) | **Care** | "Can I produce something / guide the next generation?" |
| 8 | Ego Integrity vs Despair | 65+ (maturity) | **Wisdom** | "Has my life had meaning?" — reflective acceptance |

**Notes:**
- Stage 5 was later elaborated by **James Marcia** into four *identity statuses* (diffusion, foreclosure, moratorium, achievement), cross-tabulating *crisis/exploration* against *commitment*.
- **Joan Erikson** (Erik's collaborator and widow) proposed a tentative **9th stage** in the *very* old, revisiting all earlier crises from a position of frailty, sometimes linked to Tornstam's concept of **gerotranscendence**.
- Erikson's ages are *epigenetic* and approximate, not rigid; the model is heavily Western and mid-20th-century in its assumptions about the life course.

## 3. Levinson's Adult Development — "The Seasons of a Life"

> **Framing caution:** Levinson is *often taught as an extension of Erikson* because he fleshes out the adult years Erikson compressed into three stages (intimacy → generativity → integrity). But Levinson did **not** present his work as building on Erikson — it is an independent, empirically-grounded theory (originally from in-depth interviews with **40 American men**, hence the genuine question of generalisability; *The Seasons of a Woman's Life*, 1996, came later). Pair them, but don't claim direct lineage.

### Core machinery
- **Life structure** — the underlying pattern of a person's life at a given time (work, relationships, self). The unit of analysis.
- Development **alternates** between:
  - **Structure-building (stable) periods** (~5–7 yrs) — build and live within a life structure.
  - **Structure-changing (transitional) periods** (~4–5 yrs) — question and reappraise it.
- **Eras** are ~25-year sequences that **overlap** by 4–5 years (the transitions bridge them).

### The Eras and their periods

| Era | Period | Approx. age | What's happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-adulthood** (0–22) | *Early Adult Transition* | 17–22 | Bridge out of pre-adulthood into the adult world |
| **Early Adulthood** (17–45) | Entering the Adult World *(entry structure)* | 22–28 | First adult life structure — forming **the Dream**, a **mentor** relationship, an occupation, and love/marriage/family |
| | *Age 30 Transition* | 28–33 | Reappraise the first structure; "is this the life I want?" |
| | Settling Down *(culminating structure)* | 33–40 | Establish a niche, advance; **"Becoming One's Own Man" (BOOM)** |
| | *Mid-life Transition* | 40–45 | Bridge to middle adulthood — the famous **mid-life crisis**; confronting mortality and the gap between Dream and reality |
| **Middle Adulthood** (40–65) | Entering Middle Adulthood *(entry structure)* | 45–50 | Build a new structure on revised terms |
| | *Age 50 Transition* | 50–55 | Further modification |
| | Culmination of Middle Adulthood | 55–60 | A more settled middle-adult structure |
| | *Late Adult Transition* | 60–65 | Bridge to late adulthood |
| **Late Adulthood** (60+) | Late Adulthood | 65+ | Levinson sketched this era but developed it least before his death |

### The four mid-life polarities
At the Mid-life Transition, Levinson saw the work of **individuation** as reconciling four tensions held in better balance:
1. **Young / Old**
2. **Destruction / Creation**
3. **Masculine / Feminine**
4. **Attachment / Separateness**

## Quick cross-map

| Life phase | Erikson | Levinson |
|---|---|---|
| ~17–22 | (late) Identity vs Role Confusion | Early Adult Transition |
| ~22–40 | Intimacy vs Isolation | Entering the Adult World → Settling Down |
| ~40–45 | (early) Generativity vs Stagnation | **Mid-life Transition** |
| ~45–65 | Generativity vs Stagnation | Middle Adulthood structures |
| 65+ | Ego Integrity vs Despair | Late Adulthood |

Kohlberg cuts *across* all of these — moral stage is loosely age-linked in childhood but post-conventional reasoning, where it appears at all, is an adult achievement and not guaranteed by age.
